## 1. Acceptance criteria — were they checkable, and were they checked?

    **step 5 of the same issue's "How to verify" block still carries the broken command** — the
    correction landed on one surface and not on the other, which is precisely the mechanism

## 2. Issue quality — and the release step that was skipped twice

**The issues were good. The process around them was not, and the gap is the retrospective itself.**

Grounding on all three worked issues was accurate: #409 cited the exact ordering hole in
`audit_no_verdict_merges.sh`, #425 enumerated 127 + 217 migration sites and was right, #386 was a
KPI issue whose own baseline had already been corrected at v1.14.1. No implementer had to
re-diagnose anything. The one stale citation was #425's AC6 (§1), and it was caught by running it.

**What went wrong is one level up: rule 8's retrospective step was skipped at the v1.15.0 cut and
again at the v1.15.1 cut.** Both releases tagged, both closed their issues, both ran the
release-time security check — and neither produced `docs/retrospectives/vX.Y.Z.md`. The v1.15.2
release verdict on #446 says so in writing (*"the v1.15.0 `/retro` remains outstanding and is a
mandatory release step in its own right"*), and shipped anyway. So:

- The release checklist now has **one mechanical half and one prose half**. `bump_version.sh`
  refuses a cut whose `release:vX.Y.Z` queue is non-empty (v1.15.0 change B) and it worked twice in
  this window — but nothing refuses a cut whose *previous* retro is missing. Under a keep-shipping
  directive, the prose half is what gets skipped. This is v1.15.0 §2's finding, recurring one
  release later on the adjacent step, which makes it a pattern rather than an incident.
- **§6 change C makes it mechanical** at the same place and in the same shape: `bump_version.sh`
  now also reports whether the *previous* release has a committed retrospective, so a cut cannot
  quietly be the third in a row without one.

The board is the other half of the same story, and it is measured in §5.

## 4. Review actions — 100% round-1 approvals on a window with no independent reviewer

**Every one of the 6 verdicts was written by the owner's main Claude Code session, under a standing
owner directive to work solo (no subagents). Every one of the 6 disclosed it.**

| | PRs | Round-1 approvals |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewed by an independent `pr-reviewer` agent | — | **0 of 0** |
| Reviewed by the main session (solo directive) | #437 #438 #439 #440 #441 #442 | **6 of 6** |

**The disclosure clause works: 9 of 9 verdicts across v1.15.1 and v1.15.2 carry it, 100%.**
Measured, not eyeballed — and the measurement nearly went wrong in a way worth recording, because
it is this document's own class-F near-miss: a first matcher for `not an independent` returned
**8 of 9**, because #437 writes `— **not** an independent \`pr-reviewer\` agent` and the markdown
emphasis splits the phrase. Stripping `*`/`` ` `` before matching returns 9 of 9. A regex over prose
is not a measurement until you have read what it rejected.

**So: is the gate working on easy material, or is a solo reviewer approving their own work more
readily?** The window lets both hypotheses be tested rather than asserted:

- **It is not that the PRs got small.** Median files/PR **rose** 7 → **9**, the highest since
  v1.14.0's 20, and the window contains **#440 at 89 files / 4,913 changed lines** — an order of
  magnitude above its own median. v1.15.0's falsifier for the rounds clause (median files/PR as the
  control) explicitly did **not** fire.
- **It is not that the verdicts were thin.** The six bodies run 2.3k–10.5k characters and are
  substantive: #438 re-measured eleven separate claims in a table, #439 re-ran a 35-case self-test
  and an 18-mutant contract and **proved the ledger's key property positively** (removed one line,
  watched the audit go red on that PR alone), #440 re-derived all eight acceptance criteria at the
  head including a two-directional lint mutation pin, #442 verified the new lint by *watching it
  fail*. These are not waved-through approvals.
- **And it is not that nothing was found.** Two of the six PRs carry a defect found and fixed
  before merge (#438's sort key, #439's truncation limit), and a third (#442) ships with its own
  first-draft bug as a pinned test case.

**What it is: the finding rate is real, and the *round* count is an artefact of who was holding the
pen.** A finding an author makes while reviewing their own diff costs zero rounds — it becomes
another commit before the verdict is posted, not a REQUEST CHANGES followed by a fix. The six PRs
produced at least three defects of blocker-or-major shape; under an independent reviewer at least
two of them (#438's sort key, #439's truncation guard) are exactly the kind that come back as round
one. **So `mean rounds 1.00` and `rework share 0%` do not mean the work needed no rework — they
mean the rework happened before the clock started.**

The control that settles it is the one this retro found and the window's own reviews did not:
**#435's bypass-log line and the false sentence about it in the v1.15.0 record** (§3). Self-review
caught eleven claims in a table and missed the twelfth. That is the measurable cost of the missing
arm's length, and it is why §6 change D puts the solo share in the trend table as a **column**
rather than a footnote — a 100% cell that a reader can see beside a 100% round-1 cell cannot be
quoted alone by accident.

**Which of these figures are comparable to the 14–17 PR windows?** Stated explicitly, because
v1.15.0's falsifier (a) asked for it:

| Figure | Comparable? |
|---|---|
| Median files/PR (**9**) | **Yes.** Size is per-PR by construction and the corpus is large enough to be meaningful |
| Merged with no valid APPROVE (**0**), stale approvals (**0 of 6**) | **Weakly.** A 6-PR corpus takes these to zero cheaply; quote them per-PR and beside the bypass log, never alone |
| Mean rounds (**1.00**), round-1 approvals (**100%**), rework share (**0%**) | **No.** Not because of the corpus size but because of the reviewer identity — there is no independent-review population in this window at all. Compare these cells to v1.15.2 (also 100% solo) and to nothing earlier |
| Class F / G / T counts | **No.** Every count is "what the author caught re-reading themselves" |

## 6. The changes

Enforcement order — hook > skill > charter > CLAUDE.md > command — cheapest thing that actually
prevents recurrence.

**A. `scripts/retro_metrics.sh` — the lower bound becomes the same kind of instant as the upper
one.** The script bounded the window's lower edge on the **previous tag's commit date**; the upper
edge is the release PR's `mergedAt`. Those differ by one second here (`11:09:19Z` vs `11:09:20Z`),
so **#436 — already counted by the v1.15.0 retro — appeared in v1.15.1's corpus as a seventh PR**.
The old header named the asymmetry and told the reader to eyeball it, which is exactly where a
hand-correction re-enters a document whose purpose is removing hand-counted figures. The lower
bound now accepts a **PR number** and uses that PR's `mergedAt` (`retro_metrics.sh 436 441`); the
tag form still works for back-compatibility and says which bound kind it used. **Proof in both
directions, re-run for this document:** `retro_metrics.sh v1.14.3 436` still reproduces v1.15.0's
published row exactly (6 PRs, 11 verdicts, 1.83, 50%, 45%, median 7), and `436 441` returns the
6-PR corpus above rather than 7.

**B. `lessons-learned` §79 — the class-T lesson, written where a reader will meet it.** #439's
truncation guard is the incident: a PR whose thesis is *"a permanently red alarm is a disabled
alarm"* shipped, in the same file, a mechanism that would have made that alarm permanently red in
two weeks. The lesson is the check, not the exhortation — **when a PR's argument is "X is a failure
mode", grep the same file for the second instance of X before requesting review** — and it names
the two other shapes the window produced (a control that is only accidentally right, a fix applied
to one surface of two) so the entry is reachable from any of them.

**C. `bump_version.sh` — the release cut also reports whether the PREVIOUS release has a
retrospective.** §2's finding, made mechanical at the same place v1.15.0 put the release-queue gate,
in the same shape: a **report**, not a refusal, because a missing retro must not block a hotfix, and
`RELEASE_RETRO_GATE=0` is the named escape for the deliberate case. It prints the previous version's
`docs/retrospectives/vX.Y.Z.md` as `present` or `MISSING (rule 8: the retrospective is a release
step)`. Two prose reminders in two consecutive cuts produced zero retrospectives; the queue gate
next to it produced two `empty (checked)` lines.

**D. `docs/retrospectives/README.md` — a `Verdicts by a non-independent reviewer` COLUMN, not a
footnote.** v1.15.0 recorded its 3-of-6 solo share in a footnote and in §4; this window's
round-1 rate is **100% on 100% solo verdicts**, and a footnote is not enough distance between those
two facts. Back-filled only where a release's own record measured it (v1.15.0: 3/11 verdicts;
v1.15.1: 6/6; v1.15.2: 3/3), `n-a` everywhere else — never estimated.

**E. GitHub Project 3 — the shipped items back-filled, and two release buckets corrected.**
`Review rounds` (thread-derived: 1 each), `Agent` = `main-orchestrator` (the window ran solo, by
owner directive), `Model` = `opus-5` for #386, #409 and #425; `Release` moved to the tag the work
actually shipped in for #386 and #409. `Tokens (k)` / `Time of processing (min)` deliberately left
empty: retired, and an estimate in a column headed "Tokens" is the defect note 21 exists to stop.
v1.15.2's two items are back-filled in [its own record](v1.15.2.md), not here.

**F. Issue #425's "How to verify" step 5 corrected** to the invocation that actually runs, matching
the amendment already made to its AC6. §1's finding: the correction had landed on one of the two
surfaces.

**G. The two findings deferred to issues, with the reason** (rule 11's out-of-scope carve-out — both
are a different subsystem from a documentation PR, and one needs an owner decision on scope):

1. **`pre-merge-gate.sh`'s bypass trace has two halves and only one of them fired.** #392 specifies
   a log line *and* a PR comment. On #435 the log line exists and **no comment was ever posted**;
   the comment is emitted from a detached background subshell with all output and all failures
   discarded (`( gh pr comment … >/dev/null 2>&1 & ) || :`), so a failure is indistinguishable from
   a success. An audit artefact that can silently not exist is a class-G defect in a rule-13
   control. Filed rather than fixed here as **#453**: the hook's `--mutations` contract alone
   measures ~543 s and belongs in its own PR.
2. **`check_no_pii.sh` does not cover real third-party employer names in committed fixtures.**
   #444's verdict raised it and deferred it here in as many words: the guard passed *before* the
   fixtures were scrubbed, because those strings are not on its list — *"the scrub happened by
   intent, not because the guard caught it"*. Whether the guard should own third-party company
   names is a scope decision (a portfolio's real career history legitimately contains them), so it
   is filed as **#454** with the three options costed, not unilaterally patched into a guard that
   fails closed.

**Considered and NOT changed:**

- **`pr-reviewer`** — it did not run in this window; there is nothing to teach it from six verdicts
  it did not write. Changing a reviewer charter on evidence produced by not using the reviewer would
  be the worst kind of retro edit.
- **CLAUDE.md rule 13** — the disclosure clause it gained at v1.15.0 scored **9 of 9**. It is
  working; adding to it now would dilute the one paragraph that measurably held.
- **The plugin set** — no new evidence either way. `frontend-design` stays dropped (this window's
  largest PR was a 89-file codemod with no design surface); `context7`, `pyright-lsp`,
  `typescript-lsp`, `security-guidance` unchanged. Re-reviewed per #122, recorded per the policy so
  it is not re-researched.
- **A charter paragraph about solo review** — rejected. The constraint is an owner directive, not an
  agent habit; the honest response is to make the *consequence* visible in the series (change D),
  not to instruct an agent to disobey the directive.
