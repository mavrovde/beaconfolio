## 3. Code quality — blockers clustered by class

**Predicate, stated because it changes the answer:** a PR is counted in a class when a finding of
that class was raised **at blocker level** in a posted verdict. A remark of the same shape at nit
or "rebase while you are in there" level is not counted. Each list below was re-derived from the
verdict bodies for this record, not carried over from a mid-cycle summary.

| Class | What it is | PRs affected | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C — `[Unreleased]` collision** | duplicate `### Added/Changed/Security` heading or entry after merge; one case **deleted a released heading** | #354 #357 #362 #365 #366 | **5 of 16** |
| **B — stale / CONFLICTING base** | branch behind or conflicting with `main` at review time | #354 #357 #362 #364 #365 | **5 of 16** |
| **F — claim not measured / overstated** | a claim in a permanent file contradicted by the thing it describes | #331 #362 #367 #371 #373 | **5** |
| **G — a check that cannot fail** | fake-green: lint category with no real→map direction; four masked cases in one self-test | #367 #371 | **2** |
| **E — rule 12 layer evidence missing** | wire-level behaviour with no measured local E2E | #331 #365 | **2** |
| **S — security regression introduced by the change** | `mask_quotes` prefilter turned guarded commands deny→allow | #366 | **1** |

**The overlap is the finding, not either column.** **Four of the five class-C PRs are also class
B** (#354, #357, #362, #365). The collision is not a separate defect — it is the stale base
*manifesting in `CHANGELOG.md`*, because `[Unreleased]` is the one file every concurrent branch
writes to. The union **B ∪ C is 6 of 16 reviewed PRs — 38%** (#354 #357 #362 #364 #365 #366), and
every one of them is a defect that **did not exist in the branch and did not exist in `main`**. It
existed only in the merged result, which is the state nothing validated.

That is embarrassing rather than merely unlucky, because the repository *already has the tool*:
`scripts/dedup_changelog_unreleased.py` shipped mid-cycle in #371, and `/prep-pr` step 2 documents
both halves of the check.

The gap is not knowledge. It is **enforcement level**. The tool is invoked by a
*command a human or agent must remember to run*, while the failure is produced
*mechanically* by any rebase onto a moved `main`. #365's variant was the worst: the
merge did not duplicate a heading, it **deleted the `## [1.14.0]` release section
entirely**, absorbing a shipped release's notes into `[Unreleased]`. A heading-count
check passes that.

**Two corrections to lists published earlier in the cycle**, both found by review of this
retrospective and both worth recording because the *lists* are what a future reader cites:

- **`CLAUDE.md:316` and `.claude/agents/pr-reviewer.md:62` record a different five for class B** —
  #357, #364, #365, #366, #367. That list was measured mid-cycle under a looser predicate: it
  counted #366's and #367's *"the branch is also N commits behind `main` — rebase while you are in
  there"* remarks, which were not blockers, and it predates #354 and #362, whose blocker 1 in each
  case reads verbatim *"the PR is **CONFLICTING** with `main`"*. Same count, different members. The
  count is not the useful part — **the predicate is**.
- **#373 belongs in neither B nor C.** Its round-1 verdict carries the only branch-state line in
  its whole thread — *"`0` behind `main`, 2 ahead, `mergeable=MERGEABLE`, `mergeStateStatus=CLEAN`"*
  — and its `[Unreleased]` blocker was a **false claim** (*"absent from PyPI"*, measured against the
  run log), which is class F. An earlier draft of this table had it in both B and C.

Classes C and B share one root: **a branch is validated in isolation, but the defect
only exists in the merged result.** This is the same shape as v1.14.0's Alembic head
fork, which `check_migration_heads.sh --against origin/main` now catches — and that
script is the proof the pattern is fixable at the lint level.

**Class F is the class that got worse.** At five blocker-or-major findings it is tied for the
largest, and it is the one this project's own rule 7 exists to prevent — *report what you measured,
not what you expect*. The five, each quoted from its verdict: #331 blocker 1 (*"the PR body both
state it 'moved to `.claude/PLAYBOOK.md` with content intact'. It did not"*) and major 5 (*"the
CHANGELOG entry asserts the opposite of what the PR did, and misreports a count"* — "65 cases"
against a measured 63); #362 round-2 major→blocking (*"That is the **old, wrong** list …
misdescribes security-scan coverage in the permanent record"*); #367's drift-checker claim; #371's
overstated stale-base count; #373 round-1 blocker (*"absent from PyPI"*) and the `.snyk:42`
*"from 65 to 9 ACTIONABLE findings"* measured as 8, then 5.

Four of the five landed the false claim **in `CHANGELOG.md` or a charter** — a permanent file —
rather than in code. Review caught every one, but only because a reviewer re-ran the thing the
sentence described.

## 4. Review actions — what review caught that authors missed

Every one of these was green on CI:

- a **live open redirect** surviving three fixes and three green boards (#373);
- a **new security bypass introduced by an optimisation** to the destructive guard —
  `mask_quotes` was assumed identity on quote-free input; it also de-escapes `\X` and
  blanks `#`. #366's round-1 blocker table measures **seven guarded commands flipping
  deny→allow**, one of them `echo docker\ volume\ rm\ mavrovde_db | bash` — *the #91
  incident command* — and *"18 of 23 quote-free inputs produced a different string, and 15
  of those a different decision"* (#366);
- a drift checker whose entire `lint` category **could not fail** (#367);
- a CHANGELOG tool that **silently deleted** non-whitelisted sections — `### Docs` ×6,
  `### Reverted` ×2, `### Notes`, `### Held` are all real in this file's history (#371);
- a merge that **deleted a released `## [1.14.0]` heading** (#365).

**No review round in this window found nothing** — every one of the 41 verdicts raised at
least one finding. (Stated for this release only; it has not been measured across the
v1.12.0–v1.14.1 series.) Two reviewer behaviours are worth naming as the charter working:
findings verified by **test-merge rather than by reading** (#371 blocker 1), and **mutation
used to prove a self-test can fail** — five neuterings run against the unmodified test
(#371 blocker 4).

The reviewer also corrected the *author's* overstatement of evidence (#371 major 5): a
claim of "five review rounds consumed by stale bases" was checked against all five
verdicts and reduced to the accurate "five stale-base blockers across five PRs, two of
which consumed a round and nothing else". The corrected wording is now in three
permanent files — and §3 above records that the corrected *list* still needs one more pass,
which is the same defect one level up.

## 5. Cost

2,600,168 subagent tokens across 26 runs, 361.8 agent-minutes (**6.0h**). **23 of 26 runs
(88%) were review — 2,096,091 tokens, 81% of spend, 215 of the 362 minutes.** Per merged PR:
**153k tokens.** Every figure here sums the [appendix](#appendix--per-run-effort-telemetry).

**The distribution is the finding, not the mean.** #373 (8 canonical verdicts) and #371 (4) took
**12 of 41 verdicts and 964,411 tokens on 2 of 17 PRs** — **46% of review spend, 37% of total.**
The other 15 PRs averaged **1.93** verdicts, essentially v1.14.0's 1.82. Mean rounds rose because
two PRs went badly wrong, not because the team slowed uniformly. Chasing the mean would
chase an artefact.

**One of #373's eight verdicts is an author body, and that is a hole in the counting
convention.** The canonical filter admits any posted body whose first non-empty line carries
`APPROVE` or `REQUEST CHANGES`. On #373 that admits the author's own
`## Round-2 APPROVE noted — and the head moved, so this needs a round 3` — a comment *flagging*
that an approval no longer covered the head, not a verdict. The convention's own definition says
"posted **review** bodies", so under it #373 took **7**, and the release totals become
**40 canonical / 2.35 mean / 58% rework**. The published figures keep 41/2.41/59% because that is
what the documented filter returns as run; the discrepancy is recorded in `README.md`'s
conventions so the next retro fixes the filter rather than rediscovering this. The other-15
average of 1.93 is unaffected either way.

Two waste pots were identified *and closed mid-cycle*:

- **Stale-base rounds: ~140k tokens** — two rounds that produced nothing but "rebase
  first". Now a mechanical deny at the merge gate (#375).
- **Batched reviews: 425k tokens across 4 runs**, worst single run **162,690 tokens /
  25.5 min**, with verdicts arriving late and together so fixes could not start. Banned
  by owner directive mid-cycle; later runs are one-PR-per-review and cheaper per verdict.

**Push time became a first-class cost.** Owner, 2026-09-13: *"only changed code must be
validated … simple 2 files commit for example every time takes 15 minutes"*. Filed as
#377 (P0), in implementation.

v1.14.0 set a standing prediction to be checked at v1.15. An early read against this
window:

| Clause | Target | v1.14.1 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Merges whose newest APPROVE predates a commit | 0 | **0 of 16** | ✅ |
| PRs merged with no posted verdict | 0 | **1 (#355)** | ❌ |
| Merged-result findings (fork / "green alone, broken by merge") | 0 | **6 (class B ∪ C)** | ❌ |
| Class-F blocker+major findings | ≤2 | **5** | ❌ |
| Project 3 `Review rounds` filled | all | filled for 6 of 14 by the release-manager | ⚠️ |

**The stale-approval clause passing is the release's clearest win** — 4-of-10 → 0-of-16,
attributable to `pre-merge-gate.sh` rather than to diligence.

**But it is only partly confirmed, and the limit must be stated.** The prescribed
falsifier is merge provenance, and **it cannot be established in this repository**: all
17 merges report `mergedBy: mavrovde`, because the owner and every agent share one
identity, and the API exposes no CLI-versus-web-UI distinction. The honest reading is
*"no merge that reached the gate carried an uncovered commit"* — and #355 merging with
zero verdicts is direct evidence that at least one merge did not reach it.

**The merged-result clause failed outright**, 6 times, which is what drives the first
change below.

**The class-F clause flipped from pass to fail when this retrospective was reviewed.** The
draft scored it **2 ✅** by counting only #367 and #371. Re-derived at blocker-or-major level it
is **5 ❌** (§3). Recording that here rather than quietly fixing the cell is the point: a
retrospective that scores its own prediction is exposed to exactly the class of defect it is
measuring, and this one was.

## 7. Changes — what this PR lands, and what it files

Enforcement order applied: **hook > skill > charter > `CLAUDE.md` > command.**

**Landed in this retrospective's PR:** D (telemetry, including the appendix below) and E
(documentation). **Filed as issues, because each is engineering work outside a documentation
PR's scope:** A → **#391**, B → **#392**, C → **#393**. v1.14.0's equivalent section was titled
*"Changes applied in this retro's PR"* and every entry in it was true; this section is split so
the same holds here. The next retrospective checks whether the three filed issues shipped — an
issue nobody lands is a finding deferred, not a finding fixed.

### A. Class C → a lint, not a command step *(the main change)* — filed as #391

`scripts/check_changelog_merge.sh`, run in the pre-push gate and in CI beside
`check_migration_heads.sh`, measuring the **merged result against `origin/main`**:

1. exactly one `## [Unreleased]`;
2. no duplicate `### ` heading inside it;
3. **no released `## [X.Y.Z]` heading present on `origin/main` but absent from the merge
   result** — the #365 case a heading-count check cannot see;
4. the entry diff shows only additions — a `<` line means the rebase *lost* content.

Rationale: the knowledge and the tool already existed in `/prep-pr` and still produced
blockers on 5 PRs. A command that must be remembered is not a control. This mirrors exactly
what fixed the Alembic class at v1.14.0.

### B. #355 → close the no-verdict path — filed as #392

Rule 13 admits no carve-out, yet a workflow-only PR merged with zero verdicts in two
consecutive releases (#321, #355). Establish whether the gate was bypassed or never
reached, and close that path. Until provenance is measurable, report such merges as
**unmeasured, never as a pass**.

### C. Class G → mutation contract for every new check — filed as #393

Two fake-greens this window, and *every* fake-green in this repository's history was
found by mutation, never by reading. The proposed rule, for the `pr-reviewer` charter and
`lessons-learned`: **a new or modified check must ship evidence that it fails when the
thing it guards is neutered.** The load-bearing hooks already carry `--mutations`
contracts — measured: **3 of the 4 hooks** (`guard-destructive` has none) and **0 of the 7
`scripts/*.test.sh`** lints, including `dedup_changelog_unreleased.test.sh`, where four of
this window's five fake-greens lived.

### D. Cost telemetry captured live — landed here

Recorded per-run **during** the cycle, because it is **not recoverable afterwards** — the
release-manager correctly refused to reconstruct it. It was held in a machine-local scratch file
until this review pointed out that a figure no reader can re-derive fails this repository's own
rule 7; the full per-run table is now committed as the [appendix](#appendix--per-run-effort-telemetry).
Two attribution limits stated rather than papered over: batched runs cannot be split per-issue
without inventing a ratio, and main-loop tokens are not counted.

### E. Documentation — landed here

Two wiki articles now carry the cross-release read that no single retro can:
`docs/wiki/team-and-process.md` (how the team works and why enforcement is executable)
and `docs/wiki/delivery-statistics.md` (the three-release series and the KPI plan).

Checked at the next retrospective, against the corrected baseline:

1. **Class C blocker+major findings: 0** (from 5). The direct test of change A (#391).
2. **PRs merged with no verdict at all: 0** (from 1). Test of #392.
3. **Merges on a stale approval: 0** (hold).
4. **Mean rounds ≤ 1.8** (from 2.41) **and PRs consuming ≥4 verdicts ≤ 1** (from 2 — #373 at 8

5. **Docs-only push ≤ 2 min** (from ~15), with before/after wall-clock reported in #377.
6. **Class F blocker+major findings ≤ 2** (from 5). Carried forward because it failed, and
   because #393 is not a control for it — no lint catches a false sentence in a CHANGELOG.

**Falsifiers, stated up front:**

- Round-1 approvals can rise by *approving early and merging the fixes unreviewed* —
  exactly what happened at v1.14.0, where all three round-1 approvals were also stale
  merges. Read that column **beside** the stale-approval count, never alone.
- Mean rounds can fall because PRs got trivially small. Median files/PR is the control;
  it already fell 20 → 8.
- Clauses 2 and 3 can hit zero because merges stopped passing through the gate.
  Provenance is unmeasurable here, so an unexplained zero must be reported as
  **unmeasured**, not as a pass.
- If class C goes to zero while mean rounds stay above 2.4, the constraint has moved to
  material difficulty and the answer is **not** another charter paragraph.
- Clause 1 can also hit zero because A (#391) never shipped and no PR happened to collide.
  Score it **only** alongside whether #391, #392 and #393 merged.
